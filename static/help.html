Every example below is real. This drawing was sent through Dreamer on this PC's graphics card, changing one control at a time and keeping everything else at its default. The default is outlined.

Creativity

How freely the picture is painted. Low: it stays close to what the starting image already looks like (with the default starting image, that is soft colour clouds, so it looks blurry). High: the computer invents more detail, shapes and background.

stays closeinvents more
  • Turn it downwhen pictures drift too far from the drawing.
  • Turn it upwhen pictures look washed out or unfinished.

Steps

How many times the picture is refined per frame. More steps give a little more polish, but every picture takes longer, so the screen updates less often while your child draws. The picture model is made for few steps: beyond 4 the picture mostly changes rather than improves.

Time per picture on this PC (the graphics card only; over Wi-Fi add about 40 ms). Up to 4 steps run together in one pass, which is fast. 5 to 8 run one by one, which is slower but needs no extra graphics memory. 1 or 2 steps feel live.

Follow the lines

How strongly the shapes your child drew decide where things go in the picture. Off: the drawing's shapes are ignored and only its colours and the topic count. Too high: the lines are traced as thick outlines.

loosetraces the lines

Surprise number

The same drawing and settings with another number give another picture: different details, light and arrangement. Tap New for a fresh surprise; keep a number you like.

Safety filter

Every picture is checked by a small image classifier before it is shown. If it looks unsuitable, the iPad keeps showing the previous picture instead. The prompts are already child-friendly, so this is a second line of defence. It costs about 4 ms per picture.

No examples here, for obvious reasons. If Dreamer was started with the filter off, it can only be switched on again after a restart with model.safety_checker: true.

Line sensitivity

How faint a stroke can be and still count as a line. Left in each pair: the line map the computer sees. Right: the picture. Pencil strokes on the iPad are solid, so this barely matters there.

Line thickening

Makes the lines thicker before the computer sees them. Thicker lines make bolder, simpler shapes; thin lines keep small details.

Line map

Which way round the line map is. The line-following model was trained on white lines on black, so it follows them best that way. Leave it there.

Starting image

What the picture is painted on top of. Left in each pair: the starting image. Right: the picture. Colours keeps soft clouds of your child's colours on the paper colour, so the lines guide the picture without showing up in it. Sketch starts from the drawing itself, so the strokes tend to show. Blank starts from the paper colour alone.

Colour softness

With the Colours starting image: how far your child's colours spread. Small: colours stay where they were drawn. Large: they melt into the whole area.

Picture quality

How the picture is compressed before it is sent to the iPad (zoomed in here). Lower is smaller and faster over Wi-Fi but blockier; higher is sharper but larger.
